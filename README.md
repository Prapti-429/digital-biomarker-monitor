Digital Biomarker Monitor is a research prototype that attempts to explore how multimodal digital biomarkers obtained from consumer devices may be employed for monitoring longitudinal change in the physiology of individuals.

The concept revolves around continuous and personalized health monitoring using a combination of various digital signals, including voice patterns, facial movements, head motions, respiratory patterns, and symptoms experienced by the user.
Problem Statement

Most health evaluations are carried out at clinical appointments. Slow changes in physiology between appointments are not captured and documented.

This research seeks to determine whether the use of multimodal digital biomarkers via everyday consumer technology will lead to more objective tracking of health changes than subjective reporting of symptoms.

Research Goal

To create a secure, privacy-sensitive research platform that is able to:

Capture daily multimodal health signals
Identify objective physiological attributes
Track longitudinal trends
Establish personal health baselines
Compute an interpretable health stability index
Enable future explainable AI research
